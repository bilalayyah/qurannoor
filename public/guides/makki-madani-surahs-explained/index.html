The 114 Surahs: Makki vs Madani Explained

When you open any Surah page in a printed Mushaf or inside QuranNoor, you immediately notice a label near the title marking the chapter as either "Meccan" (Makki) or "Medinan" (Madani). That single classification tells an informed reader a great deal about the historical setting, style, and moral focus of the verses about to follow.

Revealed gradually over twenty-three years rather than handed down all at once, the 114 Surahs spoke directly into two very different phases of the early community’s life—first thirteen years of struggle and spiritual foundation in Makkah, followed by ten years of community building in Madinah.

How Classical Scholars Define Makki vs. Madani

In Quranic sciences (‘Ulum al-Qur’an), the most widely used definition separates Makki and Madani chapters by time rather than strict physical geography: Makki refers to passages revealed before the Prophet Muhammad’s migration (Hijrah) from Makkah to Madinah, while Madani refers to passages revealed after the Hijrah.

Under the standard classification printed in the Cairo/Madani Mushaf tradition, 86 of the 114 Surahs are counted as Makki and 28 as Madani (a small handful of chapters sit on minor scholarly boundary discussions depending on individual verses included later, though the 86/28 count is the standard reference displayed across modern Mushafs).

Hallmarks and Themes of Makki Surahs

During the years in Makkah, believers were a small, pressured minority living within a polytheistic society that mocked the message of accountability after death. Makki Surahs therefore concentrate on the unshakeable foundations of faith: the absolute oneness of Allah (Tawhid), the reality of the Hereafter and Day of Judgment, the truth of revelation, and moral integrity.

Stylistically, Makki chapters tend to use shorter, deeply rhythmic ayahs, vivid cosmic oaths—calling attention to dawn, stars, mountains, and the human soul—and moving stories of earlier prophets such as Nuh, Musa, Ibrahim, and Yusuf to console believers facing rejection. Most chapters in Juz 29 and Juz 30 belong to this Makki phase.

Hallmarks and Themes of Madani Surahs

Once the community migrated to Madinah and established an open, plural society, revelation turned toward organizing worship, governance, social justice, and family life alongside deepening sincerity. Madani Surahs—such as Al-Baqarah (2), Ali Imran (3), An-Nisa (4), and Al-Ma’idah (5)—are generally longer chapters with calm, detailed legal and ethical prose.

Typical Madani themes include structured pillars of worship (fasting rules, zakah, Hajj), ethics of commerce and contracts, protection of orphans and family rights, sincere conduct in leadership and treaties, addressing People of the Book with wisdom, and exposing inner hypocrisy within a growing community.

Why Both Phases Matter Together for Every Reader

Notice why the Mushaf does not arrange chapters chronologically from Makki to Madani, instead placing long Madani chapters like Al-Baqarah right at the beginning while preserving Makki foundations throughout the book. Faith without ethical action grows abstract, while community laws without heartfelt certainty in Allah and the Hereafter lose their soul.

Reading across both Makki and Madani Surahs keeps heart, creed, character, and daily responsibility balanced together. When you study a longer Madani Surah alongside reading short Makki Surahs in daily prayer, you experience the complete unity of Quranic guidance across private worship and public stewardship.

Even checking the Meccan or Medinan indicator at the top of any Surah in QuranNoor before beginning your session sets helpful historical orientation in seconds. Recognizing whether verses were revealed to console resilient believers in Makkah or to guide an organized community in Madinah deepens appreciation for why the tone, rhythm, and practical examples shift so naturally across the Mushaf.

Key takeaways

  • Makki vs. Madani is primarily defined by whether a Surah was revealed before or after the Hijrah to Madinah.
  • The standard Mushaf classification lists 86 Makki Surahs and 28 Madani Surahs across all 114 chapters.
  • Makki Surahs feature concise, rhythmic verses focused on Tawhid, the Hereafter, prophetic patience, and awe of creation.
  • Madani Surahs feature longer prose passages shaping worship, family ethics, justice, charity, and community stewardship.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Makki and Madani Surahs are in the Quran?

In the widely used Mushaf classification, 86 Surahs are classified as Makki (revealed before the Hijrah) and 28 Surahs are classified as Madani (revealed after the Hijrah).

Can a Makki Surah contain any verses revealed in Madinah (or vice versa)?

Yes. Scholars note occasional individual verses inserted within a chapter from the later or earlier period, though the Surah as a whole is classified according to when its main revelation occurred.

Why are the shortest Surahs at the end almost all Makki?

The short chapters of Juz 30 were largely revealed early in Makkah, delivering vivid, unforgettable reminders of gratitude, sincerity, and accountability to the Hereafter.

Where can I see whether a Surah is Makki or Madani on QuranNoor?

Both the Surahs directory and the top header of every Surah reader page display whether the chapter is Meccan or Medinan alongside its Surah number and verse count.
